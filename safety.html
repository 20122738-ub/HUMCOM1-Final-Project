Your everyday safety toolkit

Six habits worth keeping.

Use these guides as a starting point. You can learn one topic today and come back for the next.

🔐 Protect your passwords

A different, strong password for each account helps keep one problem from spreading.

  • Use a long, unique password or passphrase.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) wherever offered.
  • Never share your password or one-time sign-in codes.
Start here: secure your email account first. It is often used to reset other passwords.

⏸ Pause before you click

Scams often use urgency, fear or an unexpected reward to make you act quickly.

  • Check the full sender address, not just the name.
  • Do not pay or enter details from an urgent message.
  • Verify unusual requests using a contact method you already trust.
If you clicked: stop, contact the service through its official channel and change exposed passwords.

📰 Check news before sharing

A convincing headline or image is not proof. Give yourself time to check the story.

  • Find the original source, author and publication date.
  • Compare the claim with reliable reporting or fact-checking.
  • Be cautious with edited images and AI-generated content.
Ask: Who made this? What is the evidence? Can another reliable source confirm it?

🔒 Keep personal information private

Share only what is needed. Small details can reveal more about you than you expect.

  • Keep your address, identity documents and banking details out of public posts.
  • Review app permissions and allow access only when needed.
  • Update your device and apps. Lock your screen on shared computers.
Before a form, check who is collecting the information, why they need it and whether the field is optional.

👥 Use social media thoughtfully

You can enjoy being connected while staying in control of your audience and time.

  • Review who can see your profile and posts.
  • Ask before posting someone else’s photo or personal details.
  • Be cautious with unfamiliar friend requests. Take a break if needed.
Before posting, would you be comfortable if this was saved or shared outside your intended audience?

💬 Know what to do about cyberbullying

Repeated hurtful messages, threats or sharing private material are not something you have to handle alone.

  • Avoid retaliating. Save evidence if safe to do so.
  • Block or mute the account and report the content.
  • Tell someone you trust. Seek urgent help if there is an immediate threat.
Listen without blame, ask what help is wanted and do not forward harmful content.

Warning signs

1. Pressure to act immediately
A deadline and a threat are meant to stop you thinking. Pause—do not let the message set the pace.

2. An unfamiliar sender address
The display name is not proof. Check the full address.

3. A request for sensitive details
A message asking for a password or payment details through a link is a warning sign.

Do not reply or click. Open the official service yourself to check your account, then report the message as phishing.
